Biography

Addie Leola Daniel was born on August 18, 1881, on Deals Island in Somerset County, Maryland. Her parents were James Daniel and Mary C. Wilson, both identified on Addie's 1922 death certificate as natives of Somerset County. FamilySearch research identifies them more fully as James Fairfax Daniel Sr. (1855–1927) and Mary Catherine Wilson (1859–1910). Addie grew up in a large Daniel family that reportedly included six siblings.

Addie married William Gabriel Snead Webster (1871–1939). FamilySearch records list their marriage as December 24, 1899, in Somerset County, Maryland, although the original marriage record has not yet been examined. The 1900 United States Federal Census records Snead Webster, age 27, and his wife Addie, age 18, living in Tangier District, Somerset County. The census gives Addie's birth as March 1882, differing from the August 18, 1881, date recorded on her death certificate.

Addie and Snead had seven known children: Maynard Snead Webster (1900–1959), Susie Catherine Webster Kramer (1902–1993), Florence Melson Webster Adams (1904–1979), Charles Daniel Webster (1909–1960), Mary Catherine Webster Jackson (1916–1995), James Wilson Webster (1920–2005), and Eugene Lee Webster (1921–1998). The family lived in Somerset County during the early years of their marriage and later established their home in Baltimore, Maryland.

Historical land records document Addie's involvement in property transactions on Deals Island. On August 20, 1906, Lazarus B. Wilson and his wife Annie B. Wilson conveyed land to Addie L. Webster for $250. Family-tree research identifies Lazarus Benjamin Wilson (1857–1922) as Addie's maternal uncle, the brother of her mother Mary Catherine Wilson. On May 12, 1909, Snead and Addie Webster conveyed land to Talmage D. Mister for $575. The later deed refers to property previously conveyed to Addie by Lazarus Wilson in 1906.

By the early 1920s, Addie and her family were living in Baltimore. Her death certificate records that she had resided in the city for approximately eight years and identifies her residence as 2602 East Fayette Street. Her death announcement in The Baltimore Sun appears to give the address as 2202 East Fayette Street, a discrepancy that remains to be resolved.

Addie died on April 26, 1922, in Baltimore, Maryland, at the age of 40 years, 8 months, and 8 days. Her death certificate identifies the cause as chronic myocarditis, a disease of the heart muscle, and names her husband, Gabriel S. Webster, as the informant. Funeral services were held on April 28, 1922, followed by burial at Woodlawn Cemetery in Baltimore. Her gravestone bears the inscription 'ADDIE L. WEBSTER, 1881–1922, MOTHER.'

Addie's death left several of her children still young, including Eugene Lee Webster, who was an infant at the time. Later federal census records show Eugene living with his older sister Florence and her husband Vernon L. Adams in 1930, 1940, and 1950. Family accounts describe the challenges faced by the Webster family following Addie's death, although the precise circumstances of the children's living arrangements during the intervening years remain under investigation.

Lazarus and Addie's mother, Mary Catherine Wilson (1859–1910), were children of Samuel David Wilson (1823–1914). The deed documents a property transaction between Lazarus and Addie, while the family relationship is identified through genealogical research and awaits independent corroboration.
