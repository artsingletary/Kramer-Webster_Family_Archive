Biography

William Daniel was born on January 24, 1826, on Deal Island in Somerset County, Maryland. He was a son of Travers Daniel (1780–1861), whose will identifies William as a son and appoints him executor of the estate. Family genealogical research identifies his mother as Mary L. Wallace (1789–1862). A contemporary newspaper biography describes his father as a farmer and successful cultivator of sweet potatoes on Maryland's Eastern Shore.

William attended Dickinson College in Carlisle, Pennsylvania, graduating in 1848. He was admitted to the bar in 1851 and began his legal career in Maryland. According to a contemporary newspaper account, he began practicing law in Baltimore in 1860.

William entered politics in 1853 when he was elected to the Maryland House of Delegates. He advocated temperance legislation and was subsequently reelected. He later served in the Maryland State Senate, where he supported local-option laws allowing communities to regulate alcohol sales.

During the Civil War, William supported the Union and became associated with the Republican Party. In 1864, he participated in Maryland's constitutional convention, which addressed the abolition of slavery in the state.

In 1872, William became president of the newly organized Maryland State Temperance Alliance. He remained a prominent leader in Maryland's temperance movement, advocating legislation that allowed individual counties to restrict the sale of alcoholic beverages.

In July 1884, William attended the national Prohibition Party convention in Pittsburgh, Pennsylvania, as head of the Maryland delegation. He served as temporary chairman and was nominated as the party's candidate for Vice President of the United States, running with presidential candidate John St. John. According to Appletons' Cyclopædia of American Biography, the St. John–Daniel ticket received 150,369 popular votes, representing approximately 1.49 percent of the national total.

William married Ella Young Guiteau, daughter of the Reverend Sheridan Guiteau and granddaughter of Dr. Thomas Kelso. Their marriage is confirmed by Ella's obituary published in the Baltimore American on March 8, 1906. Their marriage date and the names of any children remain to be established.

William lived in the Mount Washington area of Baltimore County during his later years. He died on October 13, 1897, in Baltimore, Maryland. His widow, Ella, died in 1906 and was buried at Green Mount Cemetery in Baltimore.
