Biography

James Travers Daniel was born on February 14, 1830, according to his gravestone, and spent his life in the Wenona area of Deal Island, Somerset County, Maryland. He died there on February 10, 1902, just four days before his seventy-second birthday. His death certificate confirms his death date, although the age recorded on the certificate differs slightly from the dates inscribed on his gravestone.

James received his early education on Deal Island before entering Dickinson College in Carlisle, Pennsylvania, in 1848, at approximately eighteen years of age. He attended for two years but did not graduate. According to Portrait and Biographical Record of the Eastern Shore (1898), he established a good academic record during his time at the college.

James cultivated a 20-acre farm in Wenona and operated a mercantile business near the Wenona Post Office. The 1898 biographical account described him as a businessman who adapted his activities to the needs of the surrounding community and took an interest in the welfare of its residents. His 1902 death certificate also records his occupation as a mail driver.

James was active in public affairs and supported the prohibition movement. He served as a judge of the Somerset County Orphans' Court from 1876 to 1880. According to the biographical account, he was nominated for positions in both the Maryland legislature and state senate but declined the nominations. His obituary, published in a newspaper dispatch to the Baltimore Sun, described him as a prominent Prohibitionist who had frequently represented his party in state and county conventions and had been considered for the Maryland State Senate on the Prohibition ticket.

James's brother William Daniel (1826–1897) was a Maryland attorney, state legislator, and prominent prohibition advocate who became the Prohibition Party's nominee for vice president of the United States in the 1884 presidential election, running alongside John P. St. John of Kansas. The Baltimore Sun identified William as James's brother in an article published on August 1, 1892.

Religion and community service were important parts of James's life. He was a member of the Methodist Episcopal Church, serving as a steward and trustee, and was a local preacher for fifteen years or more. He was also associated with the Heptasophs, a fraternal organization.

James married Louisa E. Rowe on January 6, 1855, in Somerset County, Maryland. Following her death, he married Elizabeth Andrews, who was also known as Lizzie Daniel. His obituary confirms that he married twice and reports that he was survived by his widow and thirteen children. The 1880 United States Census records James T. Daniel, age 50, in Deal Island in a household that also included James F. Daniel, age 24.

James's death certificate identifies his father as Travers Daniel and appears to identify his mother as Molly Wallace. These names remain subject to further verification before the parent-child relationships are formally established in the family archive.

James died at his residence in Wenona on February 10, 1902. His gravestone stands in St. John's Methodist Church Cemetery on Deal Island. Nellie Wallace Armstrong, who was approximately ten years old at the time of his death, later remembered him as a very fine man.

  7. Daniel Family — Genealogical History
    Type: family_history

    Privately compiled family genealogy titled The Daniel Name, probably prepared by Daniel Leroy Drake, son of Anna Wilson Daniel Drake and grandson of James Fairfax Daniel. The author identifies Anna Wilson Daniel as his mother, describes personally knowing James Fairfax Daniel, and references family records in his possession. The genealogy traces the Daniel family from Rowan County, North Carolina, to Deal Island, Maryland, and documents later generations of the Daniel, Drake, Webster, and related families. The latest date identified in the document is 1977, suggesting compilation or revision no earlier than that year. Copies were circulated among family members. According to family recollection, a copy was held by the Kramer-Webster family during the 1980s and certainly before 1993. The exact author attribution, compilation date, and original distribution date have not been independently verified.
