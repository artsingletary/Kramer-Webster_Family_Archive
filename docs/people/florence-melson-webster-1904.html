Biography

Florence Melson Webster was born on March 23, 1904, in Maryland, the daughter of William Gabriel Snead Webster and Addie Leola Daniel. She grew up in the Webster family, which was living in Somerset County, Maryland, in 1910 and had moved to Baltimore by 1920.

Florence married Vernon L. Adams Sr. (1899–1974), son of John H. Adams and Chessie Fitchett Adams. Their children included Vernon L. Adams Jr. and Kenneth W. Adams. The 1930 census records their older son as Lee Adams, age six, while the 1940 census identifies him as Vernon L. Adams Jr., age sixteen.

After the death of Florence's mother, Addie Leola Daniel Webster, in 1922, Florence's younger brother Eugene Lee Webster became a member of her household. The 1930 census records Eugene, age eight, living with Florence, Vernon, and their son Lee in Baltimore. John H. Adams and Chessie Adams, Vernon's parents, were enumerated in the immediately following household.

The 1940 census records Florence and Vernon with their sons Vernon Jr. and Kenneth, along with Eugene Webster, then age eighteen. In 1950, Eugene, age twenty-eight, was still living with Florence and Vernon, together with their son Kenneth. These three census records document Eugene's residence with his older sister's family over a twenty-year period, although they do not establish that he lived there continuously.

The Adams family lived in Baltimore, Maryland. The 1950 census places them on Elsrode Avenue, and Florence's death certificate identifies her final residence as 6602 Elsrode Avenue. The family's precise residential history before 1950 remains under investigation.

Florence's husband Vernon died in 1974. Florence died on April 2, 1979, in Baltimore, Maryland, at age seventy-five. She was buried on April 5, 1979, at Moreland Memorial Park in Parkville, Maryland, where she shares a grave marker with Vernon.

Florence's 1979 death notice in The Evening Sun names her surviving siblings as Susan Kramer, Catherine Jackson, and James W. Webster. The notice also names Eugene L. Webster among her children, although the family records and earlier censuses establish that Eugene was her younger brother. This discrepancy is preserved for future research.
