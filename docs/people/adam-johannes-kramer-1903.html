Biography

Adam Johannes Krämer was born on December 8, 1903, in Germany. His 1928 Declaration of Intention identifies his birthplace as Schweinfurt, while his 1942 World War II draft registration records Würzburg. His surname was generally written Kramer in American records.

Adam immigrated to the United States in 1926. According to his Declaration of Intention, he departed from Bremerhaven, Germany, aboard a vessel whose name appears to be Derfflinger, arriving in New York on or about September 29, 1926. He identified Laufach, Germany, as his last foreign residence. By April 1928, Adam was living at 937 South Kenwood Avenue in Baltimore, Maryland, and working as a seaman. According to family history, he initially lived with the family of his aunt Louise Kramer, who had married John Christopher Smearman. Louise's daughter, Charlotte 'Lottie' Smearman, is remembered in family history as the person who introduced Adam to his future wife, Sue Webster.

Adam married Susie Catherine Webster, known as Sue, on July 27, 1929, in Ellicott City, Howard County, Maryland. Their marriage was performed by H. D. Stewart of Emory Methodist Episcopal Church. According to family history, Adam and Sue were introduced by Lottie Smearman, identified as the daughter of Adam's aunt Loise Kramer. They had two children, Robert Adam Kramer and Bette Ann Kramer.

On May 19, 1932, Adam became a naturalized citizen of the United States in the United States District Court for the District of Maryland in Baltimore. His Certificate of Citizenship, No. 3523010, includes his photograph and signature.

According to family history, Adam and Sue purchased a home in Dundalk, Baltimore County, Maryland, in 1938. Their residence at 6902 Brentwood Avenue is documented on Adam's 1942 World War II draft registration and in his 1969 death notice. The family also appears in the 1930, 1940, and 1950 United States Federal Censuses.

Adam's 1942 draft registration identifies his employer as Baltimore Pure-Rye Distilling Company, located on Sollers Point Road in Baltimore. His maritime career is further documented by a United States Coast Guard Merchant Marine officer license issued on June 13, 1953, in Baltimore. The license qualified him to serve as Second Assistant Engineer of Steam Vessels of any horsepower upon any waters. The surviving license does not identify the individual ships on which he served.

Adam died on December 1, 1969, at the age of 65. His death notice identifies his residence as 6902 Brentwood Avenue in Dundalk and names his wife, Sue Webster Kramer, and their children. Funeral services were scheduled for December 4, 1969, followed by interment at Woodlawn Cemetery in Baltimore, Maryland.
